# Poker Study specification

## Purpose

Study simulated hands and reconstruct completed hands on a phone or desktop. Start with no-limit Texas Hold’em decision snapshots for two to four players (heads-up by default), using integer study chips. The user explicitly identifies each scenario as a simulation or a completed hand and starts the analysis. The product scope excludes live gambling assistance, overlays, screen sharing, background capture, table integrations, and execution of poker actions. The app cannot verify the circumstances of a manually entered hand.

## User flows

- **Table setup.** Choose 2, 3, or 4 players in the hand (default 2). Load a sample or pick cards on the table: two hero cards, a board with zero, three, four, or five cards filled from the left, and optionally two known cards per opponent. Record the pot, each player's stack behind and bet this street, and the minimum bet or last full raise increment on the table; the amount to call is shown from the biggest bet. Players who already folded are not seated; their chips are part of the pot. Verify the snapshot and select Analyze. Known opponent cards in a reconstruction are hindsight information.
- **Photo scan.** Explicitly take one still on a supporting phone or select a local image, laid out with each opponent's pair farthest from the camera, left to right in seat order (face-down pairs keep their seat), the board in the middle, and hero cards nearest. A photo showing more opponents than are seated adds seats, up to three. Preview it, then optionally press **Read cards with Claude** to send it for cloud recognition. Recognized cards land on the table flagged as unconfirmed; the user corrects or confirms every one. Enter chip and betting details manually. Unconfirmed or unresolved cards block analysis. An unknown opponent hand is intentional and gets its own model.
- **Results.** Show best five-card hand when available, win/tie/loss, split-adjusted showdown equity, analysis method and sample size, legal actions with chip costs and sizing, and an explanation of what the assumptions support. Any input edit invalidates the old result.

## Rules and state

A versioned snapshot (version 2, ruleset `holdem-nl`) carries the board, the hero's cards, stack, and street commitment, one to three opponents still in the hand (each with stack behind, street commitment, and optional known cards), the pot, and the last full raise increment or minimum opening bet. Chip amounts are integers. The starter assumes action is on the hero and raising rights are open while any opponent has chips. The amount faced is the biggest opponent commitment minus the hero's. The pot includes current wagers and dead money from earlier streets or folded players. An opponent with no chips behind and nothing committed this street went all-in on an earlier street. They cannot act, but their hand plays, and they can win only part of the pot from earlier streets: their optional `eligiblePot` (a whole number of chips, at most the earlier-street pot), or all of it when omitted. The omission is right when nobody bet beyond them after they went all-in. The table shows a "pot they can win" field for such a seat. This is a decision snapshot with no hand history or transition engine.

The plugin owns deck composition, card counts, validation, showdown ranking, action generation, and the study recommendation. Shared equity code uses the plugin deck and evaluator. A future plugin can supply other card counts and ranking policies. The starter contract supports a single high-hand winner or split among equal ranks and a community board. Extend the contract and add cross-variant fixtures before supporting stud, hi-lo, or multiple boards. Register trusted plugins in source and pin IDs to saved schema versions.

Hold’em selects the best five of the available cards. Check or fold/call are determined from the wager; full bets or raises respect the configured minimum, and a smaller aggressive action requires the hero’s entire stack. A wager needs at least one opponent with chips behind. Raise ranges are total street commitment. Showdowns pay the earlier-street pot in layers up to each earlier all-in player's limit, then this street's chips by commitment: each layer goes to the best eligible hand, split evenly on ties, and chips nobody else matched return to their owner. Chips from earlier streets or players who folded join the pots everyone still in can win. Full multiway street replay (the replay reducer stays heads-up), forced posts, reopening after short raises in multiway play, rake, and tournament ICM are later work. Use the [Poker TDA rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/) as a reference for raise increments and reopening when adding replay. Treat house policies separately.

## Hand history and replay

A hand history is a fixed setup plus an ordered list of immutable events (schema version 1). The setup holds the dealer seat, blinds, ante, and 2–10 players with starting stacks and known cards; unknown cards stay `null`. A pure reducer in `src/history/` applies one event at a time, validates each against the rules, and rejects illegal transitions without changing the prior state. The rules cover blinds and antes, heads-up and multiway action order, full and short all-in raises with reopening, uncalled bet returns, main and side pots, split pots with odd chips left of the dealer, showdown winners, and wins by fold. Chips are conserved after every event. A stored history adds the event list, the replayed final state, and the result. Import replays the events and rejects any file whose stored state disagrees. The app keeps histories in `localStorage` and replays them read-only, with step, play, speed, scrub, and timeline controls. A decision with known hero cards and two to four players can be sent to the study table. Recognition modules may only propose candidate events for the reducer to validate and the user to confirm; event inference from images is out of scope. Details: [src/history/HISTORY.md](src/history/HISTORY.md).

## Equity and recommendations

Assume fixed revealed opponent hands or uniform draws from the remaining deck for each unknown opponent. Enumerate up to 3,000 distinct outcomes. For larger spaces, run 2,500 seeded trials and draw without replacement within each trial. Report equity as wins plus split shares of ties (half heads-up, a third three ways). For samples, show a conservative 95% Hoeffding sampling bound clipped to [0, 1]. Range-model uncertainty is separate and is not captured by the bound.

For a river call or a call that puts one player all-in, with no opponent still to act on the current bet, compare expected call value with folding. Call value counts the chips the hero would collect from the main and side pots, with every opponent staying at their current commitment. Players still to act, or later streets, show pot odds only. Also show the break-even equity threshold. When the sampling interval overlaps the threshold, report an inconclusive result. Earlier calls with future betting show pot odds and withhold a recommendation. With no wager, show check as the baseline.

Bet sizing compares check (or fold and call) with candidate bets or raises at the legal minimum, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 1×, and 1.5× the pot after calling, and all-in. Sizes stop at the amount that covers the deepest opponent. It uses an explicit response model. The opponent may hold any unseen hand and ranks each holding by its equity against a random hand on this board, because they cannot see the hero's cards. Facing a bet, they call with their strongest holdings and fold the rest, never re-raising. A balanced opponent folds `to call ÷ pot after the bet`, the minimum-defense rate at which a pure bluff breaks even. "Calls too much" folds half as often, and "folds too much" folds 1.6× as often, capped at 95%. A known opponent hand calls or folds by its rank in that range. With several opponents, each one decides independently. A balanced table folds together at the minimum-defense rate, so each of the k players who can respond folds at its k-th root, scaled by style. Players already all-in stay in, and players who still had to act on an earlier bet keep their current commitment. Multiway results come from 8,000 seeded deals per decision, paid out through main and side pots, while heads-up keeps exact range weights. Expected chips assume showdown with no further betting, which is exact on the river and rough earlier. The page shows every option's fold chance, equity when called, and expected chips, names the best one for the selected style, and warns when using another style's choice would cost more than 2% of the pot. The call comparison does not rank raises, and the starter is not a solver.

Before the river, a hand outlook lists each hand type the hero can finish with: exact next-card outs, the chance of finishing with it, and, in an expandable row, the showdown result when the hero does (win, tie, loss, and the opponent hand types that beat it). It also shows the opponent's finishing hand types and how often each beats the hero. With several opponents the hero must beat the strongest of them, and the opponent panel describes that strongest hand. Boards follow the equity rules above. Matchups against a single unknown opponent are exact up to 250,000 board and hand pairs (every turn spot) and otherwise use about 100,000 seeded samples. Several unknown opponents are always sampled, and known hands are used exactly. A short note names the finishing hands that carry the most showdown equity (at least 2% of the pot, at most two) and warns about hands made at least 10% of the time that win less than half their showdowns. These are showdown odds that assume every seated player reaches the river. They ignore betting and folds, and they are not a betting recommendation. Later add weighted ranges, sensitivity analysis, and assumption-driven EV for candidate bet sizes.

## Vision and privacy

Keep pixels in browser memory and clear the preview on request, on replacement, and on closing the photo panel. Use one user-selected still, with supported raster formats and bounded file size. Keep photo bytes out of app caches, logs, telemetry, and scenario exports. No accounts.

Cloud recognition is the one exception to on-device pixels, and it is opt-in per photo. Nothing leaves the device until the user presses **Read cards with Claude** next to a notice that names the destination. The browser re-encodes the still as a JPEG of at most 1568 px on the long edge, which drops photo metadata, and posts it to the app server's same-origin `/api/recognize` endpoint. The server holds the API key, accepts only same-origin JPEG, PNG, or WebP bodies up to 5 MB, forwards the image to the Claude API (`claude-opus-5-5`, structured JSON output), and returns per-row card codes with high, medium, or low labels. The server keeps the image in memory for the request only and never logs or stores it. Anthropic's API data retention applies to the forwarded image. Without a configured key, or on a static host with no endpoint, the app reports recognition as unavailable and manual card entry still works. The on-device adapter remains available for a future local recognizer.

Recognition emits per-slot candidate cards with confidence labels, a model ID, unknown slots, and plain-language issues. The labels are the model's own and are not probabilities. Before showing any score as probability, calibrate on held-out photos grouped by deck and capture session. Choose acceptance thresholds from a measured false-accept target, including a margin between the top two candidates. Abstain on glare, occlusion, unsupported layout or deck, and ambiguous rank or suit. Flag duplicate candidates across slots. Every imported slot requires user confirmation, including high-scoring ones. Never infer hidden cards, bet sizes, turn order, or positions from the image; rows map to seats only by the user's stated layout. Report visual confidence separately from sampling uncertainty and strategy model assumptions.

### Screenshot import

Import screenshot reads one user-selected screenshot of a digital table from a simulated or completed hand. It accepts PNG, JPEG, and WebP files up to 12 MB and 200 to 16,384 pixels per side. The image is resized and re-encoded before an explicit **Read table** sends it through the same `/api/recognize` endpoint (`layout=screenshot`). Recognition returns per-field values with the reader's uncalibrated confidence label, or none, and a source: observed, inferred, or corrected. Unknown values stay unknown; only the street is inferred, from the board count, when the screen does not state it. Runtime validation rejects malformed or impossible provider output. Review checks block duplicate cards, impossible boards, conflicting streets, and impossible chip amounts, and flag unreadable or uncertain fields. Every field is correctable. Loading into the study table requires a complete, valid decision snapshot. A saved screenshot keeps its image only when the user opts in. A screenshot can be attached to a saved hand only where a replayed state agrees with everything observed; it never adds events. Live-game monitoring and play advice are out of scope. Details: [src/vision/SCREENSHOTS.md](src/vision/SCREENSHOTS.md).

### Multi-screenshot reconstruction

Reconstruct hand reads up to 20 user-ordered screenshots of one simulated or completed hand. Each screenshot has a stable ID. Order is set by the user and never taken from file names or times. Each screenshot goes through the screenshot import pipeline, at most two requests at a time. Recognized screenshots are not re-sent in a session, failures can be retried one at a time, and late results are discarded after removal or cancellation. Each result becomes an observation that keeps unknown values, confidence labels, sources, corrections, and its review status. A deterministic difference engine compares neighbouring observations. Missing values are never counted as changes. Each difference records its values, observation IDs, evidence class, confidence, and severity. Reconstruction separates confirmed facts, possible transitions (never treated as fact), and contradictions. Contradictions include changed cards, a board that shrinks, a player unfolding, a moved dealer button, changed blinds, a card in two places, and chip totals that are not conserved. Ambiguous intervals stay unresolved unless the user enters actions. The hand engine's reducer validates those actions against a checkpoint state built from the earlier screenshot; its rules are not relaxed. Only valid user-entered actions become events. The timeline navigates checkpoints and replays only confirmed actions. Sessions are saved in `localStorage` or exported as validated JSON (schema version 1). Images are stored only on request. The summary reports counts, not an accuracy percentage. Details: [src/reconstruction/RECONSTRUCTION.md](src/reconstruction/RECONSTRUCTION.md).

## Platform and interface

Use strict TypeScript, standard browser APIs, a static HTML/CSS interface, and a worker for equity. One client serves desktop and phone. A manifest and service worker provide the offline shell after a successful first load on a secure origin. Use localhost for development and HTTPS for installation on a phone. Photo capture support varies, so retain a regular file picker. Revisit a native wrapper only if device testing identifies a concrete camera, inference, or installation gap.

Use a two-column workspace on desktop, with the table editor above the results on narrow screens. The table shows one to three opponent seats, the board, the pot, and the hero seat with the amount to call; card slots open an accessible card picker that disables cards already in use. Use labeled inputs, visible keyboard focus, accessible status messages, large targets, and text for all confidence states. Use local assets and system fonts. Release testing covers keyboard, screen readers, phone layout, and offline reload.

References for the platform design include [PWA installation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Making_PWAs_installable), [service worker requirements](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Service_Worker_API/Using_Service_Workers), and [photo input compatibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Attributes/capture).
